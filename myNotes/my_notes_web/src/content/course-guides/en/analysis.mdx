Poster and worksheet applications remain. Numbered-law Boolean tasks require algebraic derivations, not truth-table substitutes.

1. Distinguish statements from sentences with free variables.
2. Specify domain and quantifiers.
3. Identify structure and implication direction.
4. Choose a proof method; justify steps.
5. Use only already-proved Boolean rules.
6. Distinguish repeated subexpressions from reusable physical gate outputs.

### Questions to answer without looking

- Why does the converse of a true implication need a separate proof?
- What is the negation of “for every $x$ there exists $y$”?
- Where does the proof of $\sqrt{2}$ use the reduced-fraction assumption?
- Why must identity laws be derived before using them to prove idempotence?
- Why does the NAND implementation of AND need two gates rather than three?

For uncertain answers, revisit the lesson. These six source documents prepare later Analysis; they do not cover limits or differentiation.


<span id="from-analysis-grundlagen-ueberblick-a-combined-problem"></span>

### A combined problem

For integers $n$, test “even $n^2$ implies $4\mid n$.” Parity gives only even $n$, not divisibility by four. Counterexample $n=2$ has even square $4$ but is not divisible by $4$. A relevant theorem may prove less than the requested conclusion.
