## Multiply the diagonal signs

<Math tex="D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n),\quad d_i\in\{-1,1\},\qquad\det D=\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_i=(-1)^k." display />

Here $n$ counts all diagonal entries, while $k$ counts only the negative ones. The product symbol means $d_1d_2\cdots d_n$. An even number of minus signs gives $+1$; an odd number gives $-1$.

<Math tex="\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1,1):\quad n=4,\quad k=2,\quad\det D=(-1)\cdot1\cdot(-1)\cdot1=1." display />

## Why an odd number is impossible

Every elementary shear has determinant 1, and so does any finite product of shears. A sign matrix with determinant $-1$ therefore cannot equal such a product. This is an obstruction, not a construction.

<Math tex="\det(T_1\cdots T_r)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\det T_j=1\ne-1." display />

## Construct a pair of sign changes

<Math tex="H=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad HVH=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=J." display />

<Flow nodes={[{"label": "Shears", "tex": "HVH", "detail": "Three permitted factors"}, {"label": "Quarter-turn", "tex": "J", "detail": "Rotate by 90°"}, {"label": "Half-turn", "tex": "(HVH)^2=-I_2", "detail": "Change both signs"}]} caption={"Read action order from the rightmost factor. The product is written in algebraic order."} />

For coordinates $i\ne j$, use $H_{ij}=I_n-E_{ij}$ and $V_{ji}=I_n+E_{ji}$. The same two-coordinate block works inside a larger matrix and leaves the other coordinates unchanged.

<Math tex="(H_{13}V_{31}H_{13})^2=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1,1)\quad(n=4)." display />

Pair the negative positions and apply one construction per pair. This proves that every sign matrix with an even number of negative entries is generated by these shears. With no negative entries, the empty product gives the identity.

<Note title={"The convention used here"}>
In these notes, “unimodular” means generated by the permitted shears, equivalently shear-equivalent to the identity. We keep that convention explicit; determinant 1 is necessary, and the construction above proves sufficiency for these sign matrices.
</Note>

<Math tex="D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1),\qquad\det D=-1." display />

<Exercise question="Can diag($-1$,$-1$,$-1$,1) be a product of shears?">
No. There are three negative entries, so the determinant is $-1$. It is still an invertible involution.
</Exercise>
