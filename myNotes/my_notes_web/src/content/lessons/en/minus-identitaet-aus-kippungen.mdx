## Exercise 9 asks for an actual product

Showing that $-I_2$ is equivalent to $I_2$ explains existence. Writing the factors in their correct order makes the construction explicit. Set:

<Math tex="L=I_2+E_{21}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad H=I_2-2E_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}." display />

| Operation | Matrix |
| --- | --- |
| $I_2$ | <Math tex="\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}" /> |
| $Z_2\leftarrow Z_2+Z_1$ | <Math tex="\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}" /> |
| $S_2\leftarrow S_2-2S_1$ | <Math tex="\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}" /> |
| $S_1\leftarrow S_1+S_2$ | <Math tex="\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}" /> |
| $Z_1\leftarrow Z_1-2Z_2$ | <Math tex="\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}" /> |


## Keep left and right multiplication in place

<Math tex="I_2\longmapsto L\longmapsto LH\longmapsto LHL\longmapsto HLHL=-I_2." display />

The first and final operations act on rows, so their factors go on the left. The middle two act on columns, so their factors go on the right. Keep each factor on its actual side when translating the operations into a product.

<Math tex="-I_2=(I_2-2E_{12})(I_2+E_{21})(I_2-2E_{12})(I_2+E_{21})." display />

<Interactive kind="matrix-product" left={[[-1,-2],[1,1]]} right={[[-1,-2],[1,1]]} />

The interactive calculation above multiplies HL by itself. Each factor HL has determinant 1, and its square is the half-turn $-I_2$, also with determinant 1.

## If only unit coefficients are permitted

<Math tex="S=I_2-E_{12},\qquad S^2=I_2-2E_{12}=H,\qquad -I_2=SS LSS L." display />

This gives six permitted unit shears. The four-factor construction uses coefficient $-2$, so keep the coefficient convention visible. Neither expression claims a minimal number of factors.

<Exercise question={"Why does a positive determinant not rule out a half-turn?"}>
A half-turn reverses both coordinate vectors. In two dimensions those two sign changes preserve orientation: $\det(-I_2)=(-1)^2=1$.
</Exercise>
