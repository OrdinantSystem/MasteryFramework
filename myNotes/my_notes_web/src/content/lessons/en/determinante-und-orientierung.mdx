## Area and sign answer different questions

The columns of a 2×2 matrix are the images of the standard basis vectors. Their parallelogram is the image of the unit square. Its area is the absolute value of the determinant; the sign records orientation.

In three dimensions, the same determinant magnitude describes volume scaling.

## Successive transformations multiply determinants

<Math tex="\det(AB)=\det A\,\det B." display />

Two reflections each reverse orientation, so their composition preserves it. In the example from the notes, two coordinate reflections make a half-turn.

<Math tex="S_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad S_y=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad S_xS_y=-I_2,\quad\det(S_xS_y)=(-1)(-1)=1." display />

<Math tex="\exists A^{-1}\iff\det A\ne0." display />

<Exercise question={"A = diag(2,−3). What happens to area and orientation?"}>
The determinant is $-6$. Area scales by 6 and orientation reverses. The matrix is invertible.
</Exercise>
