## The argument in the source

The English transcript introduces revised Bloom categories and encourages applying, comparing, and evaluating earlier in study. Its strongest practical idea is to frame a meaningful problem before collecting details. It also says higher-order work makes remembering and understanding follow almost automatically. We treat that stronger assertion as a source claim, not a guarantee.

## Where to look in the transcript

- 0:18–2:36: purpose and the taxonomy.
- 2:37–7:32: remembering, understanding, and application.
- 7:32–9:19: analysis, evaluation, and creation.
- 9:19–12:47: claims about higher-order work supporting recall.
- 12:47–14:30: memorization caveat and unsourced percentages.
- 14:30–16:16: the argument for framing a problem early.
- 16:16–17:41: comparisons and gradual changes to study.

## Our adaptation

Keep explicit recall of exact definitions and conditions. Do not turn the speaker’s rough curriculum percentages into study quotas. Give beginners enough instruction and worked examples; understanding is valuable in its own right. Use Bloom to vary the demand of a question and test the outcome, not to rank learners or declare one process universally superior.
