## Blocked and mixed practice

A page titled “Use contraposition” makes one decision before you even read the problem. That can help when you are learning the technique. Later, however, you need practice deciding whether contraposition fits at all.

<LearningFigure kind="interleaving" />

Interleaving mixes related problem types so you must recognize the relevant conditions and choose an approach. Begin with basic examples of each type if they are new. Once you can attempt them, remove the method labels and mix the order. Switching at random between unrelated subjects may add interruptions without giving you this particular practice in choosing.

## Comparison tasks

Compare the real sequences 1 − 1/n, n, and (−1)ⁿ. The first is bounded, increasing, and convergent. The second increases without bound. The third is bounded but alternates. Explaining those differences helps you see why “bounded” and “monotone” cannot be substituted for each other in a theorem.

You can vary representations, ask for a counterexample, or change a hypothesis as well. These activities complement mixed practice. The source’s broad use of “interleaving” includes such variations, but comparison, varied retrieval, and transfer are not all identical to interleaving as studied in research.

## A mixed set

Try these without first being told the method:

1. Show that the square of an odd integer is odd.
2. Decide whether every even integer is divisible by 4, and justify your answer.
3. Prove that an integer whose square is even must itself be even.

For the first, the definition of an odd integer gives a way into the algebra. For the second, a single counterexample such as 6 settles the universal claim. For the third, the first result can supply a contrapositive argument. When checking your work, explain how the form of each claim influenced your choice. If all three tasks are unfamiliar, study an example of each before attempting the mixed set.

<Exercise question="You solve a problem only after the tutor names the theorem. What would a useful next task check?">

Whether you can select the strategy. Attempt a different problem of suitable difficulty with no theorem named. State which hypotheses make your chosen result applicable. If you need another hint, record it; being able to carry out a method and being able to choose it are both worth practising.

</Exercise>
