## Blocked and mixed practice

<LearningFigure kind="interleaving" />

A block titled “use contraposition” chooses the strategy for you. A mixed set asks whether a direct proof, contraposition, or a counterexample fits. Learn basic cases first, then mix related tasks and explain why you chose each approach. Randomly switching to unrelated material is not the goal.

## Comparison tasks

For real sequences, compare 1 − 1/n, n, and (−1)ⁿ. Ask which are bounded, which are monotone, and which converge. Then change the question: which missing assumption explains a failed theorem application? Comparing, explaining, and testing boundaries complements interleaving, but these are not all the same research construct.

<Exercise question="A learner solves the problem after you name the theorem. What should the next task test?">

Strategy selection. Give a new, appropriately difficult problem without the theorem’s name. Ask which conditions matter and why another plausible method does not fit. Record whether the choice was independent.

</Exercise>

## A mixed set

Compare these tasks: show that the square of an odd integer is odd; disprove that every even integer is divisible by 4; prove that an integer with an even square is even. The first starts from a definition, the second needs a counterexample, and the third can reuse the first by contraposition. Solving them together practises recognizing their logical demands. If all three are still unfamiliar, work through an example of each before removing the method labels.
