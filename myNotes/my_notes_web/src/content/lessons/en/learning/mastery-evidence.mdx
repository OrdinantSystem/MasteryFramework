## Assessment dimensions

You may recall a definition accurately and still need help choosing it in a problem. Keep those observations separate so the next session addresses the skill that actually needs work.

<LearningFigure kind="evidence" />

The source rubric distinguishes six dimensions: precise definitions, meaningful intuition, proof or justification, boundary testing, transfer or strategy selection, and delayed retention. For each one you assess, keep the task and the answer that support your judgment. A clear analogy can support an observation about intuition, but cannot establish that a proof is valid or that you will retain it next week.

## The evidence scale

| Level | What the observation shows |
|---|---|
| NA | Not assessed |
| 0 | Not yet demonstrated in an attempted assessment |
| 1 | Success with substantial guidance |
| 2 | Partly independent performance on routine cases, with gaps |
| 3 | Accurate, justified performance without help on representative tasks |
| 4 | Flexible performance on unfamiliar variations and boundaries |

Use a level alongside its evidence, rather than as a label for the person. The six dimensions describe what was assessed; H0–H5 describes how much assistance was given. Record both when they help explain the result. Do not average the profile into a “mastery percentage”, and do not substitute zero for an assessment you have not made.

Immediate independent success remains provisional. A new task after a delay gives stronger evidence of durable learning. If you record confidence before an attempt, compare it with the result to see whether your expectations were well calibrated; confidence itself does not establish competence.

## Reading an assessment

Suppose you state a definition correctly, solve a routine example after H2, and have not yet returned to the topic. Record the definition recall as observed, the application as assisted, and delayed retention as NA. The missing delayed test is neither a pass nor a failure.

Next, choose a different problem without naming the theorem. That checks whether you can make the selection yourself. Leave another task for later. You do not need to reassess all six dimensions every day; record enough to decide what to practise next.
