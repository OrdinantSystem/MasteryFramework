## Types of record

A short note should let you resume the work without reconstructing the whole conversation. Preserve the question, your attempt, the help you used, and the next task. Link to the original work when it is too long to include.

<LearningFigure kind="handoff" />

A **session note** follows what happened on one occasion. A **concept card** gathers the precise statement, intuition and its limits, examples, proof work, and open questions for an idea you will revisit. An **error/review log** follows a particular difficulty from the first invalid step through correction, independent retry, and delayed check. You need not fill every field every time; keep what changes the next learning decision.

## Session template

<StudyTemplate kind="session" />

Record an independently solved problem separately from one completed with a hint or the answer in view. If you have not assessed something, write “not assessed”. An empty field can otherwise be mistaken for an overlooked result.

## Concept and error templates

<StudyTemplate kind="concept" />

Use a concept card for material worth returning to. Distinguish a proof copied from a source, your reconstruction after studying it, and a new argument you found independently.

<StudyTemplate kind="errors" />

In the error log, leave a correction open until a different independent attempt succeeds. A planned review date is a recommendation, not evidence that a calendar event exists. These downloads contain blank Markdown templates; save and maintain your own copies. The site does not store personal learning records.

## A useful record entry

“Reviewed logic; understood everything” leaves the next tutor guessing. Try: “Confused converse and contrapositive; used H2; corrected the $n = 6$ counterexample; new unassisted problem still pending.” Keep the original question and answer beside that note. It shows both what changed and what still needs testing.

When you start a new tutor conversation, bring the relevant note and source pointers. If a delayed test never happened, keep it marked as unassessed rather than carrying forward an assumed success.
