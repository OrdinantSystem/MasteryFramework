## A method comparison

Suppose explanations feel clear, but you still choose the wrong theorem on mixed questions. Try one change: add a small unlabeled comparison set. Before starting, define success as improved independent method selection on new, comparable tasks after a delay. Keep time and difficulty as comparable as you can.

## Results over time

Track unassisted correctness, reasoning gaps, unfamiliar transfer, delayed recall, hints, time, and fatigue. Keep the exact task and source. A good exam, a pleasant conversation, or one successful comparison cannot establish what caused the change. Repeat observations across sessions and keep, revise, or revert the change.

## Research and scope

The source collection combines established component practices with a proposed overall protocol. [Dunlosky et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/) reviewed individual learning techniques; [Karpicke and Blunt](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/) compared particular retrieval and mapping tasks. Neither study establishes that our exact workflow, timing, or six-dimensional rubric is uniquely optimal. Treat those design choices as working hypotheses.

## Interpreting an improvement

Suppose mixed practice improves your score from two correct choices to four out of five. Check whether the second set was easier, whether you saw its answers before, and whether the improvement survives a delay. Keep the exact tasks rather than just the totals. A small personal comparison can guide your next session without establishing a general educational result.
