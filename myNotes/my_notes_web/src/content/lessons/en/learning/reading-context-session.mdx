## The three prompts are different here

This Spanish transcript demonstrates a related workflow using 38 sources on learning and neuroscience. First identify central concepts and their relationships; then ask what you must understand to teach them; finally generate questions that expose superficial understanding. Unlike the other Spanish transcript, the second prompt is about teaching, not expert disagreement. Its opening MIT/90-minute anecdote remains unverified.

## The useful step is your answer

The speaker submits an incomplete answer about remembering physics formulas but failing to choose one. The feedback separates what is valid from what is superficial and points back to sources. We keep that structure, but treat the diagnosis as tentative: one answer does not establish a precise cognitive cause. Verify the claim, repair the gap, and attempt a new selection problem.

## Finish with a usable study record

The transcript requests a 90-minute plan with a task, a question, a fallback source, and an expected output for each block, plus a final ten-minute wrap. It ends with a one-page recap, typical errors, review questions, and a seven-day plan. Keep those useful outputs, while adapting the timings. Its generated neuroscience explanations are source claims to check, not independent evidence for the workflow.
