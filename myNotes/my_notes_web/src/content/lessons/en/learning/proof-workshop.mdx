## The proposition

Write the domain, quantifiers, hypotheses, and conclusion. Unpack the definitions. Try small cases to orient yourself, then look for a possible counterexample. Examples suggest a plan; they do not prove a statement about an infinite domain. Use only facts already allowed in the course.

## Levels of assistance

<LearningFigure kind="hints" />

Write your next logical step before asking for the tutor’s. Record the highest hint used. If you needed a local step (H3), the completed proof is assisted work. Afterwards hide the solution and reconstruct it; later try a changed hypothesis, a converse, or another example.

<Exercise question="You checked 100 positive integers. Have you proved the claim for every natural number?">

No. A finite sample does not cover the infinite domain. Find a general argument using the definitions and permitted results. An exhaustive check is a proof only when the domain is finite and every permitted case is covered.

</Exercise>

## An appropriate first hint

For “the sum of two odd integers is even”, an H1 hint might ask what the definition of oddness supplies. H2 names the representation 2k + 1. H3 writes the two inputs as 2r + 1 and 2s + 1. These supports reveal progressively more of the proof. After using H3, a new independent task could ask about the sum of three odd integers. Repeating the supplied lines alone does not establish independent strategy selection.
