## Preparation

Choose a bounded topic, an observable goal, your available time, and the sources you trust. Begin with two or three diagnostic questions or a short problem. If an essential prerequisite is missing, teach or review it before increasing difficulty.

## Session structure

1. Diagnose what you can do without help.
2. Make a small provisional map and explain its links.
3. Move from an example to the precise definition or theorem.
4. Attempt problems, with support gradually removed.
5. Diagnose a specific error and retry a different case.
6. Finish with closed-notes recall and a new application.
7. Leave a short record and a delayed review task.

## Time and difficulty

The source files offer 75-, 90-, and 120-minute examples. These are planning examples, not required durations. Spend more time on independent proofs when proofs are the goal; shorten mapping when the relationships are already clear. If fatigue makes the attempts unproductive, stop and leave a precise next task.

## Example session

For “prove claims about even and odd integers”, begin by writing their definitions without notes. If those are missing, review them before proving anything. Next reconstruct the odd-square argument, then attempt an even-square implication without the method being named. Finish by explaining why n = 6 refutes a divisibility converse. The session record should distinguish the argument you reconstructed after seeing it from the new problem you solved alone.
