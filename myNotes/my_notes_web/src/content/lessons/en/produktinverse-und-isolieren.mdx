## Undo the last action first

In ABx, B acts before A. To restore x, apply the inverse of A first, then the inverse of B. This gives the order of the inverse product. All matrices in these identities are square and invertible of the same size.

<Math tex="(AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}." display />

<Math tex="(AB)(B^{-1}A^{-1})=A(BB^{-1})A^{-1}=I_n,\qquad(B^{-1}A^{-1})(AB)=B^{-1}(A^{-1}A)B=I_n." display />

<Math tex="(ABC)^{-1}=C^{-1}B^{-1}A^{-1}." display />

## Exercise 11: isolate the inverse without commuting

<Math tex="MAN=I_n,\qquad \exists A^{-1},M^{-1},N^{-1}." display />

Multiply the equation on the left by $M^{-1}$ and on the right by $N^{-1}$. Associativity allows regrouping, not reordering.

<Math tex="A=M^{-1}N^{-1}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\colResult{A^{-1}=NM}." display />

You can check both sides directly. From MAN = I, first obtain $MA=N^{-1}$ and $AN=M^{-1}$.

<Math tex="(NM)A=N(MA)=NN^{-1}=I_n,\qquad A(NM)=(AN)M=M^{-1}M=I_n." display />

## An additional numerical check

This example uses the shears from the previous lesson. It also shows why replacing NM by MN is unsafe.

<Math tex="M=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad N=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}." display />

<Math tex="MAN=I_2,\qquad NM=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad MN=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}." display />

<Exercise question={"If MAN = I, which side is used to cancel N?"}>
Multiply on the right by $N^{-1}$. The rightmost pair becomes $NN^{-1}$, leaving $MA=N^{-1}$.
</Exercise>
