## Laws 1–5

<Math tex="\tag{1}(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})\lor \colC{C}=\colA{A}\lor(\colB{B}\lor \colC{C}),\quad(\colA{A}\land \colB{B})\land \colC{C}=\colA{A}\land(\colB{B}\land \colC{C})" display />

<Math tex="\tag{2}\colA{A}\lor \colB{B}=\colB{B}\lor \colA{A},\quad \colA{A}\land \colB{B}=\colB{B}\land \colA{A}" display />

<Math tex="\tag{3}\colA{A}\land(\colB{B}\lor \colC{C})=(\colA{A}\land \colB{B})\lor(\colA{A}\land \colC{C})" display />

<Math tex="\colA{A}\lor(\colB{B}\land \colC{C})=(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})\land(\colA{A}\lor \colC{C})" display />

<Math tex="\tag{4}\colA{A}\lor(\colA{A}\land \colB{B})=\colA{A},\quad \colA{A}\land(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})=\colA{A}" display />

<Math tex="\tag{5}\colA{A}\lor\neg \colA{A}=1,\quad \colA{A}\land\neg \colA{A}=0" display />

The numbers denote associativity, commutativity, distributivity, absorption, and complement. Both dual forms are allowed. Here $=$ means the expressions are logically equivalent, not that their written shapes match. Substitute whole expressions for $A$, $B$, or $C$ when applying a law.

Associativity changes the grouping of the same operation. Distributivity changes the tree and repeats $A$. In the exercise sheets, truth tables are not the proof method: use these five laws to derive the later rules.

## Recognizing the shape of a law

In $A \land  (B \lor  C)$, $A$ is combined with a choice between $B$ and $C$. Distributing asks for the two possible ways the conjunction can hold: $A$ with $B$, or $A$ with $C$. The repeated $A$ is essential. For absorption, $A \lor  (A \land  B)$ already accepts every case where $A$ holds; the additional case requires $A$ anyway and adds nothing. These readings explain the identities; the worksheet derivations must still use the allowed laws.
