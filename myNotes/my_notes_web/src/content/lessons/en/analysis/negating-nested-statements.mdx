## Negating nested statements

Separate readability fails at one student unable to read any shelf book; shared readability fails with a possibly different student per book. Separate successes need not yield a shared book.


<span id="from-negating-nested-statements-work-outside-in-on-a-finite-domain"></span>

### Work outside in on a finite domain

For $D=\{1,2\}$, $\forall x\in D\;\exists y\in D\;(y>x)$ fails at two: neither $y$ is larger. Negate the inner existence as a unit:

$$
\begin{aligned}\neg(\forall x\in D\;\exists y\in D\;(y>x))&\Longleftrightarrow\exists x\in D\;\neg(\exists y\in D\;(y>x))\\&\Longleftrightarrow\exists x\in D\;\forall y\in D\;\neg(y>x)\\&\Longleftrightarrow\exists x\in D\;\forall y\in D\;(y\le x).\end{aligned}
$$

Select a failing input; no witness works, so every candidate fails. Not greater includes equality: fix two, test one and two.

<span id="from-negating-nested-statements-start-with-existence-instead"></span>

### Start with existence instead

$$
\neg(\exists x\in D\;\forall y\in D\;(y<x))\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall x\in D\;\exists y\in D\;(y\ge x).
$$

Strictly above every member includes itself, unlike a non-strict maximum. Allowed $y=x$ defeats every $x$ and witnesses denial. Empty domain: original false, denial vacuously true; no outer universal cases require witnesses.

Over reals, adding one makes the first claim true, its negation false. Domain changes truth, not logical pattern; symbolic negation does not prove failure.

## Negating quantified statements step by step

Deny “Every applicant has a document passing every check” outside inward: fix a failing applicant; each document fails some check. Simultaneous word swaps can obscure fixed objects.

Fix $A$ applicants, $B$ documents, $C$ checks; $R(x,y,z)$ means $y$ passes $z$ for $x$. Start with full negation:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\neg(\forall x\in A\;\exists y\in B\;\forall z\in C\;R(x,y,z))\\\Longleftrightarrow\;&\exists x\in A\;\neg(\exists y\in B\;\forall z\in C\;R(x,y,z))\\\Longleftrightarrow\;&\exists x\in A\;\forall y\in B\;\neg(\forall z\in C\;R(x,y,z))\\\Longleftrightarrow\;&\exists x\in A\;\forall y\in B\;\exists z\in C\;\neg R(x,y,z).\end{aligned}
$$

First fix a failing applicant; then exclude successful documents; finally supply a failed check per document. Checks may vary by document, not one required for all.


<span id="from-negating-quantified-statements-step-by-step-why-each-replacement-is-legitimate"></span>

### Why each replacement is legitimate

Each whole inner predicate’s dual law preserves truth for **every outer-variable assignment**. Unchanged surrounding quantifiers therefore preserve equivalence; repetition proves the chain. All lines may be false: refuting the original additionally requires proving the final failure condition.


<span id="from-negating-quantified-statements-step-by-step-a-method-that-respects-scope"></span>

### A method that respects scope

1. Bracket exactly the denied scope; outside quantifiers stay unchanged.
2. Record variable order, domains and fixed parameters; free occurrences stay free.
3. Switch one quantifier and negate its whole remainder.
4. Repeat, preserving order, relation arguments and domains: books stay shelf-dependent, positive reals positive.
5. Apply each connective’s negation rule and negate the final predicate.
6. Read back; check each witness’s permitted earlier information.

$$
\neg(A\Rightarrow B)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A\land\neg B.
$$

Signed-and-failing refutes “if signed, passes”; unsigned cannot refute it.
