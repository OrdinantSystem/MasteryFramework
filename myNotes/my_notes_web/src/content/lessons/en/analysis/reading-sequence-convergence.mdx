## Reading sequence convergence

The device’s complete rule gives nine twice, then three forever. Convergence allows early exceptions but requires every sufficiently late term to stay arbitrarily close to one fixed target.


Before the definition, distinguish the index from the term and distance from signed displacement. Revisit [sequences and distance](../real-sequences/#from-real-sequences) if either is uncertain. In the picture, move the threshold past the early exceptions: the rule specifies every later value, not merely the visible dots.

### Fix the objects before reading the quantifiers

Fix real sequence $a:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R$, $\mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$, and real target $L$. Term $a_n$ at index $n$ has distance $|a_n-L|$. Context fixes $a,L$; no written quantifier below binds them.

$\varepsilon$ is a variable positive real tolerance, not an index. Positive natural $N$ starts the tail; positive natural $n$ tests it. $n\ge N$ includes the threshold; $|a_n-L|<\varepsilon$ excludes equality.


<span id="from-reading-sequence-convergence-the-positive-definition"></span>

### The positive definition

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists N\in\mathbb N\;\forall n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon\bigr).
$$

<Flow nodes={[{"label": "Tolerance", "tex": "\\varepsilon>0", "detail": "Accept any positive real tolerance."}, {"label": "Threshold", "tex": "N\\in\\mathbb N", "detail": "Choose one threshold using the tolerance."}, {"label": "Tail test", "tex": "n\\ge N", "detail": "Keep the threshold fixed for every test."}]} caption="A tolerance, then a threshold, then every tail index." />

To prove convergence, verify this definition: accept any tolerance; choose $N$ using it and fixed context, then test every index with $N$ fixed. Tolerance dependence is permitted, not required.

Earlier indices have false hypothesis, hence no closeness requirement. Every tail term must pass, not just its first. Larger natural thresholds work by tail containment; no smallest is required.

For the device and target three, threshold three gives zero distance on its entire tail for every tolerance. Exact attainment is not required for convergence.

## Proving sequence convergence

Fix sequence and target before the arbitrary tolerance; changing targets for individual terms would change the claim.


<span id="from-proving-sequence-convergence-the-full-constant-sequence-proof"></span>

### The full constant-sequence proof

Let $a_n=3$ throughout, $L=3$. For arbitrary $\varepsilon>0$, choose natural $N=1$. Every natural $n\ge N$ satisfies:

$$
|a_n-3|=|3-3|=|0|=0<\varepsilon.
$$

Rule, subtraction, absolute value at zero and $\varepsilon>0$ justify the chain. Every tail index passes for arbitrary tolerance. No natural unboundedness or triangle inequality is needed.


<span id="from-proving-sequence-convergence-choice-dependence-and-proof-dependence"></span>

### Choice dependence and proof dependence

Choice dependence permits tolerance and fixed context, not later $n$. Proof dependence concerns the estimate’s justification. Correct choice order cannot repair false arithmetic, and correct arithmetic cannot repair a witness chosen after the test index. Constant $N$ may ignore tolerance.



<Note title="A late threshold changes the claim">

After each test, $N=n+1$ makes its implication vacuously true but supplies no single tail threshold. Forbidden dependence on later $n$, not size, is the error.

</Note>

Use the definition above to check the order: tolerance, threshold, every tail index.

<Exercise question="A sequence has $a_1=a_2=7$ and $a_n=-2$ for every $n\ge3$. Prove convergence to $L=-2$ for arbitrary positive tolerance. Choose the threshold before the test index. Must your threshold depend on the tolerance?">

Fix $L=-2$ and accept arbitrary $\varepsilon>0$. Choose $N=3\in\mathbb N$. For every natural $n\ge N$, the sequence rule gives $a_n=-2$, hence $|a_n-L|=|-2-(-2)|=0<\varepsilon$. Thus every term of this tail passes every positive tolerance. The same threshold works for all tolerances; it need not be smallest. Choosing $N=n+1$ after the test index would not supply one threshold for the entire tail.

</Exercise>

Check your proof: fixed sequence and target; arbitrary positive tolerance; a natural threshold chosen without the later test index; an estimate covering every index in that tail. If you used a hint or the solution, try a changed sequence later without looking.

<span id="from-reciprocal-sequence-convergence"></span>

## Reciprocal sequence convergence

Reciprocal terms approach zero without reaching it. Supply a threshold for every tolerance and check its tail.

<Note title="Optional extension: additional accepted background">

Optional, not required for [logical negation](../negating-sequence-convergence/#from-negating-sequence-convergence). Accept without proof: **Archimedean unboundedness**, every real bound is exceeded by some positive natural number; **positive reciprocal order**, increasing a positive denominator decreases or preserves its reciprocal. Quantifier notation proves neither fact.

</Note>


<span id="from-reciprocal-sequence-convergence-find-a-threshold-then-prove-its-tail-works"></span>

### Find a threshold, then prove its tail works

$$
a_n=\frac1n\quad(n\in\mathbb N),\qquad L=0.
$$

All terms are defined and positive. For arbitrary real $\varepsilon>0$, $1/\varepsilon$ is real. Archimedean unboundedness gives positive natural $N>1/\varepsilon$. Fix it; any natural $n\ge N$ satisfies:

$$
|a_n-0|=|1/n|=1/n\le1/N<\varepsilon.
$$

The rule and subtracting zero give the first equality; positivity removes absolute value. Reciprocal order gives $1/n\le1/N$. From $N>1/\varepsilon$, multiply by positive $\varepsilon$, then divide by positive $N$, obtaining $1/N<\varepsilon$. Thus the whole tail passes every arbitrary tolerance, proving convergence.

Positive terms never equal the limit. The accepted facts cover infinitely many tolerances and indices, unlike any finite list.

<span id="from-reciprocal-sequence-convergence-a-strict-boundary-check"></span>

### A strict boundary check

At $\varepsilon=1/10$, $N=11$ works since later distances are at most $1/11<1/10$. $N=10$ fails by equality at its own term. This illustrates strictness only.

<span id="from-reciprocal-sequence-convergence-read-aloud"></span>

### Read aloud

Beyond capital N greater than one over epsilon, every reciprocal is at most one over capital N, hence below epsilon.


<Exercise question="Optional transfer: with the same accepted arithmetic background, prove that $b_n=2/n$ converges to zero. Explain threshold existence and the estimate for every later index.">

For arbitrary $\varepsilon>0$, Archimedean unboundedness gives $N\in\mathbb N$ with $N>2/\varepsilon$. Fix it. For every $n\ge N$, positivity and reciprocal order give $|b_n-0|=2/n\le2/N<\varepsilon$. The last strict inequality follows by multiplying $N>2/\varepsilon$ by positive $\varepsilon$ and dividing by positive N. This checks the whole tail; the extra arithmetic facts are separate from quantifier order.

</Exercise>
