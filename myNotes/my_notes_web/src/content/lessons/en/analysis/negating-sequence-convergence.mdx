## Negating sequence convergence

First derive failure at one target, not absence of all real limits. Fix sequence and target.

Recall $\varepsilon>0$ as tolerance, $N$ as threshold, $n$ as test index. Convergence to fixed real $L$ means:

$$
\forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists N\in\mathbb N\;\forall n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon\bigr).
$$

A fixed threshold must work for its entire tail, not earlier indices. These six equivalent negations alone establish no particular sequence’s failure.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-1-deny-the-whole-definition"></span>

### 1. Deny the whole definition

$$
\neg\Bigl(\forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists N\in\mathbb N\;\forall n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon\bigr)\Bigr).
$$

Negate the complete statement without changing its interior or fixed context.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-2-cross-the-tolerance-quantifier"></span>

### 2. Cross the tolerance quantifier

$$
\exists\varepsilon>0\;\neg\Bigl(\exists N\in\mathbb N\;\forall n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon\bigr)\Bigr).
$$

Some positive tolerance defeats the remainder. Positivity is retained.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-3-cross-the-threshold-quantifier"></span>

### 3. Cross the threshold quantifier

$$
\exists\varepsilon>0\;\forall N\in\mathbb N\;\neg\Bigl(\forall n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon\bigr)\Bigr).
$$

For this fixed tolerance, every natural threshold fails.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-4-cross-the-test-index-quantifier"></span>

### 4. Cross the test-index quantifier

$$
\exists\varepsilon>0\;\forall N\in\mathbb N\;\exists n\in\mathbb N\;\neg\bigl(n\ge N\Rightarrow |a_n-L|<\varepsilon\bigr).
$$

Each threshold needs one falsifying index, which may depend on it and fixed context. Not every index must fail.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-5-deny-the-implication"></span>

### 5. Deny the implication

$$
\exists\varepsilon>0\;\forall N\in\mathbb N\;\exists n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\land\neg(|a_n-L|<\varepsilon)\bigr).
$$

False implication means true hypothesis and false conclusion at the same index. Retain tail membership and conjoin distance failure; pre-tail terms cannot refute it.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-6-include-equality-in-distance-failure"></span>

### 6. Include equality in distance failure

$$
\exists\varepsilon>0\;\forall N\in\mathbb N\;\exists n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\land |a_n-L|\ge\varepsilon\bigr).
$$

Not below $\varepsilon$ means at least $\varepsilon$, including equality. One positive tolerance defeats every proposed tail through a term at least that far from $L$, not through failure of every late term.


<span id="from-negating-sequence-convergence-prove-one-actual-failure"></span>

### Prove one actual failure

For constant $a_n=0$, $L=1$, take $\varepsilon=1$. Every natural $N$ permits $n=N\ge N$ with $|a_n-1|=1\ge\varepsilon$. Yet target zero succeeds for every $\varepsilon>0$ with $N=1$ and zero distance. This sequence has a real limit.

## No real limit

No real limit requires failure at every real target for the fixed sequence, not just one.

Abbreviate the complete positive convergence definition by $C(L)$. A real limit means $\exists L\in\mathbb R\;C(L)$; negate that outer existence:

$$
\neg(\exists L\in\mathbb R\;C(L))\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall L\in\mathbb R\;\neg C(L).
$$

Substitute the derived failure condition. Tolerance may depend on the earlier target but stays fixed across all its thresholds.

$$
\forall L\in\mathbb R\;\exists\varepsilon>0\;\forall N\in\mathbb N\;\exists n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\land |a_n-L|\ge\varepsilon\bigr).
$$

No real limit, or real-sequence divergence, need not mean growth or tending to infinity. One tolerance for all targets would be stronger and need justification. The bad index may use target, threshold, tolerance and sequence.

### A proof for every target

For $a_n=(-1)^n$, fix an arbitrary real $L$ and choose $\varepsilon=1/2$. Let any $N\in\mathbb N$ be proposed. If $L\ge0$, take an odd $n\ge N$: then $a_n=-1$ and $|a_n-L|=1+L\ge1$. If $L<0$, take an even $n\ge N$: then $a_n=1$ and $|a_n-L|=1-L>1$. One of N and N+1 has the required parity. Each proposed tail therefore contains a term whose distance is at least $\varepsilon$. Since L was arbitrary, no real target is a limit. The argument covers indices beyond the plotted sample.

<span id="from-no-real-limit-a-quantifier-swap-can-destroy-the-test"></span>

### A quantifier swap can destroy the test

$$
\forall N\in\mathbb N\;\exists\varepsilon>0\;\exists n\in\mathbb N\;\bigl(n\ge N\land |a_n-L|>\varepsilon\bigr).
$$

It wrongly rechooses tolerance after thresholds and omits equality at the same tolerance. Negation changes binder types in place, not order.

Optional countermodel: [reciprocal convergence](../reading-sequence-convergence/#from-reciprocal-sequence-convergence), accepting Archimedean background. Although $a_n=1/n$ converges to zero, each $N$ permits $n=N$, $\varepsilon=1/(2N)$, with $1/N>1/(2N)>0$: the wrong-order condition holds. The main route needs only constant proofs.


<span id="from-no-real-limit-optional-nuance-a-separately-equivalent-strict-form"></span>

### Optional nuance: a separately equivalent strict form

With correct order $\exists\varepsilon>0\;\forall N\;\exists n$, strict failure is separately equivalent: distance at least $\varepsilon$ implies distance above positive $\varepsilon/2$ at the same bad terms. Conversely, greater implies at least. Changing the witness proves equivalence; it does not make $\neg(d<\varepsilon)$ mean $d>\varepsilon$ at fixed epsilon. Halving cannot repair swapped quantifiers.
