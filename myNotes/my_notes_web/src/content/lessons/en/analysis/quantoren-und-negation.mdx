## A witness versus every case

Existence needs one witness. A universal claim covers every element of its stated domain; one counterexample refutes it. ∃! means exactly one, combining existence and uniqueness.

<Math tex="\exists x\in\mathbb N:x+3=10\quad\text{(}x=7\text{)}\qquad\neg\bigl(\forall x\in\mathbb N:x+3=10\bigr)" display />

## Push the negation inward

<Math tex="\neg(\forall x\in D:P(x))\iff\exists x\in D:\neg P(x)" display />

<Math tex="\neg(\exists x\in D:P(x))\iff\forall x\in D:\neg P(x)" display />

Negation changes the quantifier and the predicate, while keeping the domain. With nested quantifiers, change each one in order. Their order matters: one common y for every x is stronger than a possibly different y for each x.

<Math tex="\neg(\forall x\,\exists y:P(x,y))\iff\exists x\,\forall y:\neg P(x,y)" display />

## The boundary case matters

The claim n² > n for every natural number fails at n = 1. With ℕ starting at 1, the correct universal statement is n² ≥ n; strict inequality holds for n > 1.
