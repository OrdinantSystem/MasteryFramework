## 7c and 7d · The absorbing values

<Math tex="\colA{A}\lor1\overset5=\colA{A}\lor(\colA{A}\lor\neg \colA{A})\overset1=(\colA{A}\lor \colA{A})\lor\neg \colA{A}\overset6=\colA{A}\lor\neg \colA{A}\overset5=1" display />

<Math tex="\colA{A}\land0\overset5=\colA{A}\land(\colA{A}\land\neg \colA{A})\overset1=(\colA{A}\land \colA{A})\land\neg \colA{A}\overset6=\colA{A}\land\neg \colA{A}\overset5=0" display />

Do not confuse neutral and absorbing values. OR with 0 preserves A; OR with 1 forces 1. AND with 1 preserves A; AND with 0 forces 0. Idempotence (6) is now available from the previous proof.

## 8 · Negating constants

<Math tex="1\overset5=0\lor\neg0\overset2=\neg0\lor0\overset{7a}=\neg0" display />

<Math tex="0\overset5=1\land\neg1\overset2=\neg1\land1\overset{7b}=\neg1" display />

The conclusions are $\neg 0 = 1$ and $\neg 1 = 0$. Each equality follows from a numbered law; no truth table or De Morgan rule has been assumed.

## Neutral and absorbing elements

Think of $A$ as an unknown truth value. $A \lor  0$ still depends on $A$, while $A \lor  1$ is settled whatever $A$ is. For $\land$ the roles reverse: 1 leaves $A$ unchanged and 0 settles the result. In the proof of $A \lor  1 = 1$, replacing 1 with $A \lor  \neg A$ brings a repeated $A$ into view. Associativity puts the copies together; idempotence removes one. This explains why law 6 must already be available.
