## Sheet 1.2 · a complement is unique

<span id="from-eindeutiges-komplement-both-conditions-are-needed"></span>

### Both conditions are needed

<Math tex="\colA{A}\lor \colB{B}=1,\quad \colA{A}\land \colB{B}=0,\qquad \colA{A}\lor \colC{C}=1,\quad \colA{A}\land \colC{C}=0" display />

Assume $B$ and $C$ both complement A. The OR condition covers all possibilities; the AND condition excludes overlap. Use one candidate’s conditions to simplify the other.


<span id="from-eindeutiges-komplement-reduce-both-to-the-same-expression"></span>

### Reduce both to the same expression

<Math tex="\begin{aligned}\colB{B}&\overset{7b}=\colB{B}\land1=\colB{B}\land(\colA{A}\lor \colC{C})\\&\overset3=(\colB{B}\land \colA{A})\lor(\colB{B}\land \colC{C})\\&\overset2=0\lor(\colB{B}\land \colC{C})\overset{2,7a}=\colB{B}\land \colC{C}\end{aligned}" display />

<Math tex="\begin{aligned}\colC{C}&\overset{7b}=\colC{C}\land1=\colC{C}\land(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})\\&\overset3=(\colC{C}\land \colA{A})\lor(\colC{C}\land \colB{B})\\&\overset2=0\lor(\colC{C}\land \colB{B})\overset{2,7a}=\colC{C}\land \colB{B}\overset2=\colB{B}\land \colC{C}\end{aligned}" display />

Thus $B = C$. The unnumbered substitutions use the assumed complement conditions. Since $\neg A$ is a complement by law 5, any expression satisfying both conditions must equal $\neg A$. This is the tool for the next proofs.


<span id="from-eindeutiges-komplement-why-compare-bbb-with-ccc"></span>

### Why compare $B$ with $C$?

We know how $B$ relates to $A$ and how $C$ relates to $A$, but not yet how $B$ relates to $C$. Insert $1 = A \lor  C$ into $B \land  1$ to connect the two sets of information. Distribution separates the part $B$ shares with A—which vanishes—from $B \land  C$. Repeating the argument for $C$ shows that both candidates equal their overlap. Only one complement can therefore satisfy both requirements. Merely satisfying $A \lor  B = 1$ would not suffice: $B = 1$ always does that.

<span id="from-de-morgan-und-doppelte-negation"></span>

## Sheet 1.2 · De Morgan and double negation

<span id="from-de-morgan-und-doppelte-negation-9a--a-complement-of-aba-lor--bab"></span>

### 9a · A complement of $A \lor  B$

Set $X = A \lor  B$ and $Y = \neg A \land  \neg B$. Show $X \land  Y = 0$ and $X \lor  Y = 1$ using only the previously available laws.

<Math tex="\begin{aligned}X\land Y&\overset{2,3}=(\colA{A}\land\neg \colA{A}\land\neg \colB{B})\lor(\colB{B}\land\neg \colA{A}\land\neg \colB{B})\\&\overset{1,2,5}=(0\land\neg \colB{B})\lor(0\land\neg \colA{A})\\&\overset{2,7d}=0\lor0\overset6=0\end{aligned}" display />

<Math tex="\begin{aligned}X\lor Y&\overset3=[(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})\lor\neg \colA{A}]\land[(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})\lor\neg \colB{B}]\\&\overset{1,2}=[(\colA{A}\lor\neg \colA{A})\lor \colB{B}]\land[\colA{A}\lor(\colB{B}\lor\neg \colB{B})]\\&\overset5=(1\lor \colB{B})\land(\colA{A}\lor1)\\&\overset{2,7c}=1\land1\overset6=1\end{aligned}" display />

<Math tex="\neg(\colA{A}\lor \colB{B})=\neg \colA{A}\land\neg \colB{B}" display />

Uniqueness identifies $Y$ with $\neg X$. That proves the first De Morgan law; it was not used inside its own proof.


<span id="from-de-morgan-und-doppelte-negation-10--double-negation-independently"></span>

### 10 · Double negation independently

<Math tex="\neg \colA{A}\lor \colA{A}=1,\quad\neg \colA{A}\land \colA{A}=0,\qquad\neg \colA{A}\lor\neg\neg \colA{A}=1,\quad\neg \colA{A}\land\neg\neg \colA{A}=0" display />

By laws 2 and 5, $A$ and $\neg\neg A$ are both complements of $\neg A$. Uniqueness gives $\neg \neg A = A$. This argument does not require De Morgan.


<span id="from-de-morgan-und-doppelte-negation-9b--derive-the-second-law"></span>

### 9b · Derive the second law

<Math tex="\neg(\colA{A}\land \colB{B})\overset{10}=\neg(\neg\neg \colA{A}\land\neg\neg \colB{B})\overset{9a}=\neg\neg(\neg \colA{A}\lor\neg \colB{B})\overset{10}=\neg \colA{A}\lor\neg \colB{B}" display />

In the middle step apply 9a backward with the inputs $\neg A$ and $\neg B$. The proof order is uniqueness → 9a and 10 → 9b.


<span id="from-de-morgan-und-doppelte-negation-the-complement-strategy"></span>

### The complement strategy

To identify $\neg (A \lor  B)$, we do not assume that negation distributes. Instead propose a candidate $Y$ and check the two properties that uniquely identify a complement. The first chain proves that $X$ and $Y$ never hold together; the second proves they cover every case. This is why both chains are needed. For double negation the same method is shorter: compare the two complements of $\neg A$ directly. The second De Morgan identity then follows from already established results, avoiding a circular argument.


<span id="from-de-morgan-und-doppelte-negation-negating-a-compound-claim"></span>

### Negating a compound claim

The proved identities now let us transform a negated statement without building a truth table.

<Exercise question="Negate “A holds and at least one of B or C holds”.">

The statement is $A \land  (B \lor  C)$. Its negation is $\neg A \lor  \neg (B \lor  C)$, hence $\neg A \lor  (\neg B \land  \neg C)$. The inner OR becomes AND when negated; merely negating all three letters would miss the grouping.

</Exercise>
