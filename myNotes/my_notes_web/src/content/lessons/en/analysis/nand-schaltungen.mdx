## One operation

<Math tex="\colA{A}\mid \colB{B}:=\neg(\colA{A}\land \colB{B})" display />

NAND gives $0$ only for two $1$ inputs. Symbols | or ↑ mean NAND here, unlike divisibility $a | n$. A gate’s output circle means negation.

## Three constructions

<Math tex="\neg \colA{A}=\colA{A}\mid \colA{A}\qquad \colA{A}\land \colB{B}=(\colA{A}\mid \colB{B})\mid(\colA{A}\mid \colB{B})" display />

<Math tex="\colA{A}\lor \colB{B}=(\colA{A}\mid \colA{A})\mid(\colB{B}\mid \colB{B})" display />

NOT ties $A$ to both NAND inputs. AND feeds one NAND output to both inputs of another: two physical gates despite the repeated term. OR negates both inputs, then NANDs them: three gates. Constructing NOT, AND and OR makes NAND functionally complete.


<span id="from-nand-schaltungen-following-one-signal"></span>

## Following one signal

For OR with $A=1$, $B=0$, the first gates give $\neg A=0$, $\neg B=1$; the final NAND negates $0\land1$, giving $1$. With $A=B=0$, both first outputs are $1$, giving final $0$. De Morgan and double negation prove the general identity $\neg(\neg A\land\neg B)=A\lor B$. One signal may feed multiple inputs without rebuilding its gate.
