## One operation

<Math tex="\colA{A}\mid \colB{B}:=\neg(\colA{A}\land \colB{B})" display />

NAND outputs 0 only when both inputs are 1. Its symbol may be written | or ↑. Here | denotes NAND, while $a | n$ in the earlier number-theory example denoted divisibility: context matters. In a circuit, the small circle on the output means negation.

## Three constructions

<Math tex="\neg \colA{A}=\colA{A}\mid \colA{A}\qquad \colA{A}\land \colB{B}=(\colA{A}\mid \colB{B})\mid(\colA{A}\mid \colB{B})" display />

<Math tex="\colA{A}\lor \colB{B}=(\colA{A}\mid \colA{A})\mid(\colB{B}\mid \colB{B})" display />

NOT connects A to both inputs of one NAND. AND reuses the first NAND output at both inputs of a second gate: two physical gates, even though the expression repeats a term. OR first negates each input, then NANDs the results: three gates. Since NOT, AND, and OR can all be constructed, NAND is functionally complete.

## Following one signal

Take $A = 1$ and $B = 0$ in the OR circuit. The first two gates produce $\neg A = 0$ and $\neg B = 1$. The final NAND negates $0 \land  1$, so its output is 1. With $A = B = 0$, the first gates both output 1 and the final output is 0. These traces explain the construction; the identity follows because $\neg (\neg A \land  \neg B) = A \lor  B$ by De Morgan and double negation. One signal may feed several gate inputs without requiring the original gate to be rebuilt.
