## A compact route through the material

The Analysis 0 lessons provide the slower route through these foundations. Start with [statements and predicates](../aussagen-und-aussageformen/), then [definitions](../definitions-assumptions-results/), [equality](../equality-and-equations/) and [order comparisons](../order-comparisons/). [Logical connectives](../logische-verknuepfungen/), [negation](../logical-negation/) and [building truth tables](../building-truth-tables/) prepare the [conditional](../material-implication-and-vacuity/) and [equivalence](../biconditionals-and-equivalent-conditions/) lessons.

For quantifiers, read [universal claims](../universal-statements-and-domains/), [scope](../bound-variables-and-scope/), [existence](../existence-and-witnesses/), [order and dependence](../quantifier-order/) and the [negation procedure](../negating-quantified-statements-step-by-step/). The [sequence-convergence application](../reading-sequence-convergence/) uses those ideas after introducing [sequences](../real-sequences/) and [absolute distance](../absolute-value-and-distance/). Its [negation derivation](../negating-sequence-convergence/) distinguishes failure at a specified target from [having no real limit](../no-real-limit/).

The original poster and worksheet lessons remain useful applications. In particular, the numbered-law Boolean exercises below require their stated algebraic proof route; an earlier truth-table explanation does not replace that task.

1. Decide whether the sentence is a statement or still contains free variables.
2. Specify the domain and quantifiers.
3. Identify the logical structure and direction of the claim.
4. Choose a proof method and justify each step.
5. For Boolean derivations, keep track of which rules have already been proved.
6. For circuits, distinguish a written subexpression from a physical gate whose output can be reused.

## Questions to answer without looking

- Why does the converse of a true implication need a separate proof?
- What is the negation of “for every $x$ there exists $y$”?
- Where does the proof of $\sqrt{2}$ use the reduced-fraction assumption?
- Why must identity laws be derived before using them to prove idempotence?
- Why does the NAND implementation of AND need two gates rather than three?

Return to the corresponding lesson when an answer feels uncertain. These foundations support later Analysis topics; limits and differentiation are not covered by these six documents.

## A combined problem

Consider “for every integer $n$, if $n^2$ is even then $n$ is divisible by 4”. First identify the domain and implication. The parity lemma gives only that $n$ is even, which is weaker than the claimed conclusion. At $n = 2$, $n^2 = 4$ is even but 4 does not divide $n$. This one case refutes the universal statement. A familiar theorem can be relevant without proving everything the problem asks.
