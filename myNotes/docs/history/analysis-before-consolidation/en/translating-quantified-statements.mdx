“Every student has read a book” leaves the book available for each student. “There is a book every student has read” requires one shared book. To write a specification, define the objects and relation before choosing symbols.

Let $S$ be students, $B$ books and $R(s,b)$ mean that $s$ has read $b$. Keep that relation unchanged; do not silently switch from reading to ownership.

$$
\forall s\in S\;\exists b\in B\;R(s,b).
$$

$$
\exists b\in B\;\forall s\in S\;R(s,b).
$$

Read back the first as “for each student, a book they have read”; the second as “a book read by every student”. Neither requires exactly one book. The first does not assert that students exist. With no students it is true; the second still requires an actual book.

## Write, then read back

Specify each domain and the final relation. Decide which requirement governs the rest, then write the binders and their scopes. Check every variable: bound here or fixed in context? Finally read the formula back and test a small situation where a tempting alternative differs. Translation and proof are separate tasks. Preserve a false assertion faithfully instead of changing its order to make it true.

$$
\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\forall y\in\mathbb R\;\exists z\in\mathbb R\;(z=x+y).
$$

This translates “Every pair of reals has a real sum”. Both inputs precede the witness. Its truth additionally uses closure of real addition; the quantifier symbols alone do not prove that arithmetic fact.

## A conditional limits the requirement

$$
\forall s\in S\;\bigl(A(s)\Rightarrow\exists b\in B\;R(s,b)\bigr).
$$

Here $A(s)$ means attending the seminar. Only attending students must supply a read book. Moving $\exists b$ outside the conditional can demand a book for nonattenders too. Take one nonattending student and no books: the displayed conditional is true, but $\forall s\in S\;\exists b\in B\;(A(s)\Rightarrow R(s,b))$ is false. Parentheses and empty domains expose the difference. Restricting $S$ explicitly to attenders is another faithful translation.

### Practice

With people $P$, keys $K$ and usability $H$, translate “Each person can use a key”.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Person first.

</details>

<Exercise>

$\forall p\in P\;\exists k\in K\;H(p,k)$. The key may depend on the person; distinct choices are not required.

</Exercise>

Translate “A key can be used by every person” in the same language.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Keep one key fixed.

</details>

<Exercise>

$\exists k\in K\;\forall p\in P\;H(p,k)$. One fixed key must work for every person; it need not be unique.

</Exercise>

Translate and prove “Every real has a strictly larger real”.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Introduce the input before the witness.

</details>

<Exercise>

$\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\exists y\in\mathbb R\;(y>x)$. For arbitrary $x$, choose the real sum $y=x+1$; positivity of one makes it larger.

</Exercise>

Translate “A real number equals every real number”, then evaluate.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Do not alter a false assertion.

</details>

<Exercise>

$\exists y\in\mathbb R\;\forall x\in\mathbb R\;(y=x)$. False: any proposed $y$ fails at real $x=y+1$.

</Exercise>

Translate “For every real $x$ there is a real $y$ such that for every real $z$, $x+y=z$”.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Preserve all three layers.

</details>

<Exercise>

$\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\exists y\in\mathbb R\;\forall z\in\mathbb R\;(x+y=z)$. The witness may use $x$, not later $z$. False: one fixed sum cannot equal both zero and one.

</Exercise>

Later, write both book translations from memory and read them back.

## Read aloud

**EN:** For every x in D, there exists y in E such that R of x and y holds.

**DE:** Für jedes x in D gibt es ein y in E, sodass R von x und y gilt.

**ES:** Para todo equis en D existe un ye en E tal que se cumple R de equis, ye.
