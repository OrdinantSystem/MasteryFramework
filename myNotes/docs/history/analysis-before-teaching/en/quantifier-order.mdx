## Reading mathematical structure

“Find a real below two” instructs; “every real is below two” asserts falsely. Identify a sentence’s job before its symbols.


<span id="from-reading-mathematical-structure-a-reading-procedure"></span>

### A reading procedure

Identify definition, assertion, assumption, question or instruction. Check domains, definedness (no real division by zero), free/bound occurrences and contextual values. Preserve order and scope: $\forall$ means every, $\exists$ at least one, not unique. Parse outermost first with complete inputs; distinguish whole hypothesis and conclusion without inventing hypotheses for equalities. Parsing, evaluation and proof differ.

In $x+y=3$, addition combines numbers, not propositions. A quantified statement can be one propositional input letter without being first-order atomic.


<span id="from-reading-mathematical-structure-a-universal-claim-with-a-conditional-inside"></span>

### A universal claim with a conditional inside

Translate “Every real strictly between zero and one is below two”:

$$\forall x\in\mathbb R\;(((0<x)\land(x<1))\Rightarrow(x<2)).$$

Both strict bounds form the hypothesis.

The quantifier binds all $x$ in the whole conditional, making it closed. Inside, $\Rightarrow$ (or $\to$) joins the entire conjunction to $x<2$; $\land$ means “and”, not another outer connective.

Keep both $0<x$ and $x<1$ when translating, even if proof needs only the latter. No interval membership is asserted for all reals.

Both parts hold at $x=1/2$, fail at $x=3$. A counterexample needs **whole hypothesis true**, conclusion false; three is ineligible.

For arbitrary eligible real $x$, $x<1<2$ gives $x<2$ by transitivity. This proves, unlike translation or samples alone, using accepted classical semantics and real order.


<span id="from-reading-mathematical-structure-practice-in-both-directions"></span>

### Practice in both directions

<Exercise question="Parse $x=0\lor((x>0)\land(x<1))$. Give the outer connective, its two whole inputs, and the free variables. Then translate ‘x equals zero and it is not the case that y equals zero’.">

Outermost $\lor$ joins $x=0$ to the whole $(x>0)\land(x<1)$; all $x$ stay free without a binder. Translation: $(x=0)\land\neg(y=0)$, negating only the second equality, with both variables free.

</Exercise>

Translate “Every real above one exceeds zero”; identify its hypothesis and prove it without samples. Are all reals asserted positive?

</details>

<details>

## Quantifier order

Complete model: Ana uses only red, Ben only blue. Each has a key; none works for everyone.

| Person | Red | Blue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ana | yes | no |
| Ben | no | yes |

$P$ denotes people, $K$ keys, $H(p,k)$ usability. Each quantifier governs the whole remainder.

$$
\forall p\in P\;\exists k\in K\;H(p,k).
$$

Each person has a key: red for Ana, blue for Ben. Choices may differ, need not.

$$
\exists k\in K\;\forall p\in P\;H(p,k).
$$

Red fails Ben, blue Ana: no fixed key works for all. Nested quantifiers lie in another’s scope, not merely conjoined statements. Both variables are bound; the first governs the second. Order is logical, not time or an algorithm.

<span id="from-quantifier-order-the-same-distinction-over-the-reals"></span>

### The same distinction over the reals

$$
\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\exists y\in\mathbb R\;(y>x).
$$

True: arbitrary real $x$ allows $y=x+1$, real by closure and larger by positivity of one. This covers every input.

$$
\exists y\in\mathbb R\;\forall x\in\mathbb R\;(y>x).
$$

False: every proposed $y$ fails at allowed $x=y$, requiring $y>y$. The rule $y=x+1$ illegally changes the fixed witness with a later input.


<span id="from-quantifier-order-safe-swaps-have-conditions"></span>

### Safe swaps have conditions

Fixed independent $D,E$ allow adjacent universals to commute: applying the original requirements to any ordered pair proves both directions. Both are vacuously true if either domain is empty. Existentials commute via a successful pair; both are false if either domain is empty. Keep relation arguments $R(x,y)$ unchanged. Dependent $E(x)$ cannot precede $x$.

<span id="from-witness-dependence"></span>

## Witness dependence

With $y=x+1$, $x$ must be available first. Track available inputs across three quantifiers.


<span id="from-witness-dependence-keep-an-information-ledger"></span>

### Keep an information ledger

Witnesses may use fixed parameters, earlier inputs whose scopes contain them, and earlier witnesses, never later universal inputs. Dependence is optional: positive one can ignore inputs. Existence promises no computability.

$$
\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\forall y\in\mathbb R\;\exists z\in\mathbb R\;(z=x+y).
$$

Both inputs are available: $z=x+y$ is real by closure and satisfies the equality for every real pair.

$$
\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\exists z\in\mathbb R\;\forall y\in\mathbb R\;(z=x+y).
$$

$z$ may use $x$, not later $y$: $y=0$ forces $z=x$, $y=1$ forces $z=x+1$. Incompatibility refutes every fixed candidate, not merely samples.

An envelope choice may use an earlier key: existential choices coordinate. Fixed real $c$ is available in $y=x+c$ but syntactically free without a binder. Dependence is not binding: bound witnesses using earlier inputs remain bound.

Check information, membership and full predicate separately: allowed $y=1$ fails $y>x$ at $x=1$; allowed $y=x/2$ fails integer membership there.

## Separate and shared witnesses

Adding green for both makes both forms true. Two master keys give alternative shared witnesses: fixed does not mean unique.

$$
\underbrace{\exists k\in K\;\forall p\in P\;H(p,k)}_{\text{S}}\ \Rightarrow\ \underbrace{\forall p\in P\;\exists k\in K\;H(p,k)}_{\text{T}}.
$$

Assume S; fix its key. Universality makes it work for any person; reuse proves T without distinct choices. Red–blue refutes the converse.

Shared implies separate, not opposite truth values: green makes both true, someone without any key both false. A complete finite permission table can establish these claims; samples from an infinite domain do not cover all cases.

<span id="from-separate-and-shared-witnesses-all-empty-domain-cases"></span>

### All empty-domain cases

| People | Keys | Separate T | Shared S |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| empty | nonempty | true | true |
| nonempty | empty | false | false |
| empty | empty | true | false |

No people makes separate vacuous; shared requires an existing key, then vacuous universality. People without keys lack witnesses. S implies T throughout: true S supplies the key; false S makes implication true.

[Real strict comparisons](../quantifier-order/#from-quantifier-order) separate the forms. For $y\ge x$, separate uses $y=x$; shared still fails, now at $x=y+1$, not $x=y$.

<span id="from-translating-quantified-statements"></span>

## Translating quantified statements

Translate the key distinction to “Every student has read a book” versus “There is a book every student has read”.

$S$ denotes students, $B$ books; $R(s,b)$ means reading, never silently ownership.

$$
\forall s\in S\;\exists b\in B\;R(s,b).
$$

$$
\exists b\in B\;\forall s\in S\;R(s,b).
$$

First: read book per student; second: one read by all. Neither means unique. Without students the first holds, without asserting students exist; the second still needs a book.


<span id="from-translating-quantified-statements-write-then-read-back"></span>

### Write, then read back

Specify domains, relation, binders and scopes; distinguish bound from context-fixed variables. Read back and test a small separating case. Translate false claims faithfully, without confusing translation and proof.

$$
\forall x\in\mathbb R\;\forall y\in\mathbb R\;\exists z\in\mathbb R\;(z=x+y).
$$

“Every real pair has a real sum”: both inputs precede the witness. Truth additionally requires closure of addition, not quantifiers alone.


<span id="from-translating-quantified-statements-a-conditional-limits-the-requirement"></span>

### A conditional limits the requirement

$$
\forall s\in S\;\bigl(A(s)\Rightarrow\exists b\in B\;R(s,b)\bigr).
$$

$A(s)$ means attendance: only attenders need a read book. With one nonattender and no books, this is true but $\forall s\in S\;\exists b\in B\;(A(s)\Rightarrow R(s,b))$ false: moving $\exists b$ demands a book even for nonattenders. Restricting $S$ to attenders is also faithful.
