## The three prompts are different here

This Spanish transcript demonstrates NotebookLM with 38 sources on learning and neuroscience. It first asks for central concepts and their relationships, then asks what someone would need to understand to teach the material, and finally requests questions that expose superficial understanding. The second question concerns teaching; the other Spanish transcript asks about expert disagreements. Keeping that difference matters when adapting the prompts.

The opening MIT/90-minute story is unverified here. The file supplies headings, but no reliable timestamps, author, or original video URL. Its generated neuroscience explanations should be checked against the cited sources rather than treated as independent evidence for the workflow.

## The useful step is your answer

One generated question describes a learner who remembers physics formulas but cannot choose one for a problem. The speaker answers that the learner needs more real exercises. The demonstrated feedback accepts part of that answer, asks what is missing, and points back to sources.

That feedback structure can help, but one answer cannot establish a precise cognitive cause. The learner might misunderstand a quantity, miss an assumption, or struggle with strategy selection. Ask them to explain the relevant quantities and choose between two plausible formulas, giving reasons. Their next attempt helps decide which gap needs work. The classification remains tentative until you have enough evidence.

## Finish with a usable study record

The transcript asks for a 90-minute plan with a task, a question to answer, a fallback source, and an expected output for each block, plus a final ten-minute wrap. You can preserve those planning fields while adapting the timing to the learner and topic.
