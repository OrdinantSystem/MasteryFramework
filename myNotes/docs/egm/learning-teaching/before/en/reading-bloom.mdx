## The argument in the source

The English transcript recommends bringing application, comparison, and judgment into study earlier. Instead of collecting definitions with no use in sight, begin with a meaningful problem so you can see why the details matter.

<LearningFigure kind="problem-first" />

The speaker also argues that higher-order work makes remembering and understanding follow almost automatically. That is a stronger source claim, not a guarantee adopted here. The supplied file does not identify the author or original video URL.

## Where to look in the transcript

- 0:18–2:36 introduces the purpose and revised taxonomy.
- 2:37–7:32 contrasts remembering, understanding, and application.
- 7:32–9:19 discusses analysis, evaluation, and creation.
- 9:19–12:47 claims higher-order work supports recall almost automatically.
- 12:47–14:30 allows some memorization and gives unsourced curriculum percentages.
- 14:30–16:16 argues for framing a problem before studying details.
- 16:16–17:41 recommends comparison and gradual changes to study.

## Our adaptation

Use an early problem as an orientation. For sequence convergence, it can show why you need to distinguish a bound from a limit. If the learner does not yet know either term, explain them and work through an example before demanding an independent proof.

Keep explicit retrieval of definitions and theorem conditions. The transcript’s rough percentages for how much a curriculum needs memorization do not provide study quotas. Bloom describes the demand of a task; it does not prove that one category is always more effective or that understanding is of little value. Choose tasks that address the learning goal, then inspect what the learner can do without help and after a delay.
