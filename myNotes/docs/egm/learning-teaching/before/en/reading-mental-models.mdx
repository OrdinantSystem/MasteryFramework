## Three questions shape the workflow

This Spanish transcript opens with the story of an MIT student allegedly mastering a subject in 48 hours rather than five months. It then demonstrates NotebookLM prompts using a personal-finance collection. The story’s outcome and any MIT endorsement remain unverified here. The supplied file has headings but no reliable timestamps, author, or original video URL.

The demonstration asks for shared mental models, points of expert disagreement, and questions that expose shallow understanding. Its proposed use is to organize the sources, examine differences, then attempt the questions and investigate failed answers.

## Adapt the questions to university work

1. Ask which definitions and relationships support the topic, with actual source locations. Requesting exactly five models shared by all experts can pressure a system to invent consensus. Accept fewer, more, or uncertain connections when the sources warrant them.
2. Compare assumptions, methods, and interpretations where they genuinely differ. In mathematics, two valid proof approaches or a theorem and its false converse often provide a better comparison than an invented expert dispute.
3. Attempt a new diagnostic problem before reading its answer. Ask the tutor to identify the first specific gap, and check the correction against the source.

For example, a source-grounded comparison can ask why a linear map must send zero to zero, then whether the converse holds. You must justify one direction and find a counterexample to the other. Reading a generated comparison without making that attempt leaves your own reasoning untested.

## Follow-ups and limits

In “Exprime el sistema”, the speaker asks how the models connect and requests a source-specific plan for the learner’s gaps. Such a plan needs your actual answers; without them, a system can only guess what you understand.

“Crea tus propios recursos” demonstrates reports, presentations, and audio. Check these generated materials before using them, and leave time to explain or solve something yourself. Uploading more sources does not by itself make a claim reliable. The financial examples, generated advice, and sponsorship are not recommendations from this course.
