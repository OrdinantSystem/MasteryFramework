## A method comparison

Suppose explanations make sense, but you still choose the wrong theorem on mixed questions. Try adding a small set of problems whose headings do not name the method. Before you begin, write down what improvement would look like: more correct, independent method choices on new, comparable tasks after a delay.

Change one part of the routine when practical. Keep study time and task difficulty as comparable as you can, and decide how to check the answers before you see the results. That makes the comparison easier to interpret than changing the tutor, timetable, and exercises together.

## Results over time

Keep the tasks as well as the scores. Note unsupported proof steps, transfer attempts, delayed results, hints, active study time, and fatigue when relevant. A method that improves one immediate score but creates an unsustainable workload may need adjustment.

Repeat the comparison across sessions, alternating the order of approaches when feasible. Then decide whether to keep, revise, or reverse the change. One good exam or enjoyable conversation cannot tell you which part of a routine caused the outcome.

## Research and scope

The source collection combines research-supported individual practices with a proposed overall workflow. [Dunlosky et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/) reviewed learning techniques; [Karpicke and Blunt](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/) compared particular retrieval and concept-mapping tasks. Neither establishes that our complete sequence, timings, or six-dimensional rubric is uniquely optimal. Those remain design choices to examine against actual learning.

## Interpreting an improvement

Imagine that you move from two correct method choices to four out of five. Before crediting mixed practice, inspect the tasks: perhaps the second set was easier, or you had already seen similar answers. Check again after a delay with new tasks covering comparable demands.

A small personal comparison can help you choose the next session’s work even when it cannot establish a general educational result. Write the limited conclusion the observations support, together with the uncertainty that remains.
