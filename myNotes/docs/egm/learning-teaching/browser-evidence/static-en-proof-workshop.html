Learning methodology/The study workflow/Proof Practice

Proof Practice

PrerequisitesStudy Sessions

The proposition

Before looking for a clever argument, write down exactly what must be proved. Identify the domain, quantifiers, assumptions, and conclusion. For “the sum of two odd integers is even”, the domain is the integers, the assumption concerns each of the two summands, and the conclusion concerns their sum.

Unpack the definitions and try small cases to get a sense of the claim. Also look for a counterexample: a false statement needs correction before it needs a proof. For a true statement, list the definitions and earlier results you are allowed to use. This can reveal a route without borrowing the very result you are trying to establish.

Levels of assistance

Try writing your next step before asking for one. H0 records an attempt without hints; H1 offers a strategic question; H2 names a relevant concept or theorem; H3 supplies a local step; H4 gives an analogous worked example; H5 supplies the complete proof. Ask for more help when you need it and record the highest level used. An H3-assisted proof is useful practice, but does not establish that you could find the argument alone.

Afterwards, audit each inference and check that the conclusion answers the original quantified claim. Hide the solution and reconstruct it, then try a variation. Delayed work on a changed task gives stronger evidence than immediate repetition.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

You checked 100 positive integers. Have you proved the claim for every natural number?

Show solution +

No. There are still natural numbers outside the sample. You need an argument covering an arbitrary permitted value. A finite exhaustive check can prove a claim when the domain itself is finite and every allowed case is covered; checking only a sample does not do that.

An appropriate first hint

For the odd-sum claim, H1 might ask: “What does knowing an integer is odd let you write?” If you need H2, the tutor can point to the representation 2k+12k + 12k+1. At H3, the two summands are written as 2r+12r + 12r+1 and 2s+12s + 12s+1, leaving you to combine them and justify the conclusion.

Complete the guided two-odd argument before checking this reference.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Show solution +

Write the two odd integers as a=2r+1a=2r+1a=2r+1 and b=2s+1b=2s+1b=2s+1, with integers r,sr,sr,s. Then a+b=2(r+s+1)a+b=2(r+s+1)a+b=2(r+s+1). Since r+s+1r+s+1r+s+1 is an integer, the sum is twice an integer, hence even.

After that proof, determine whether the sum of three odd integers is even or odd, and prove your answer without hints. Keep the variables’ integer status explicit. The new task checks whether you can choose and use the representation again, rather than merely reproduce the supplied lines. This is an immediate variation after a guided example; record that exposure separately from hints used on this attempt.

Write a=2r+1a=2r+1a=2r+1, b=2s+1b=2s+1b=2s+1, and c=2t+1c=2t+1c=2t+1, with integers r,s,tr,s,tr,s,t. Their sum is a+b+c=2(r+s+t+1)+1a+b+c=2(r+s+t+1)+1a+b+c=2(r+s+t+1)+1. The expression r+s+t+1r+s+t+1r+s+t+1 is an integer, so the sum is odd. Separate parameters allow the three odd integers to differ.
