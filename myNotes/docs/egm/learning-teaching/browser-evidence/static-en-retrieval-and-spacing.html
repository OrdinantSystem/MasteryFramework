Retrieval and Spaced Practice

PrerequisitesLearning Methodology

Retrieval before checking

You may recognize every line of a proof while reading and still be unable to write its first step with the page closed. To find out what you can retrieve, hide the notes and attempt a definition, explanation, or problem before checking the answer.

If the attempt fails, open the source and study the missing part. Reading has a useful job here: it supplies or corrects knowledge you could not produce. Then close it and try again. Keep track of whether an answer came from memory or from the page in front of you.

You can reread on several days without retrieving, or retrieve repeatedly in one sitting without spacing across days. Recalling one definition does not require mixing problem types.

The worked example in Mind Maps follows a learner who confuses retrieval with feedback. It separates the unaided attempt, source comparison, correction, reconstruction, and changed application. Repeating the corrected explanation is reconstruction; a different task provides a separate check.

Review intervals

The source protocol suggests brief reviews on the same day, the next day, after 3–4 days, around a week, and after 21–30 days. Use these as starting examples, not a timetable you must obey. Shorten a gap after difficulty and lengthen it after reliable independent success. Deadlines and a manageable workload matter too.

Change the task as well as the date. Recall a theorem’s assumptions in one review; later solve a problem without its name in the heading. That helps distinguish remembering a familiar answer from having knowledge available for use.

Flashcard design

A card works best when it asks one clear question and gives you an answer you can check. Exact definitions, theorem conditions, brief relationships, small counterexamples, and short applications can fit. Make cards after you have begun to understand the material, and rewrite cards that have become vague or overloaded. Keep full proofs and longer problems in your problem-solving practice.

Practice testing and distributed practice received high utility ratings in Dunlosky et al.’s 2013 review. That supports the component practices, without validating our exact review intervals or complete workflow.

Primary experiments also separate immediate from delayed outcomes: Roediger & Karpicke (2006) found restudy favored five-minute prose recall, but prior testing favored recall after two days or one week. In fact learning, Cepeda et al. (2008) found the best study gap varied with the final-test delay. Neither fixes your next interval or validates this complete routine.

Example: a card and a problem

Replace “Do I understand contraposition?” with “State the contrapositive of A⇒BA \Rightarrow BA⇒B.” You can check the answer ¬B⇒¬A\neg B \Rightarrow \neg A¬B⇒¬A. A different card can ask for a counterexample to “every even integer is divisible by 4”; 6 works because it is even but not divisible by 4.

Neither card asks you to decide when contraposition would be useful. Leave a separate, unlabeled proof task for that decision. If you recall the card easily but cannot choose the method, add selection practice rather than simply increasing repetitions of the same card.

A study decision

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

A learner rereads cards repeatedly in one evening. Next day they recall the definitions but cannot choose which concept fits a new case. Propose the next attempt, feedback, and later check. Explain what each examines; you are planning practice, not solving a subject task here.

Show solution +

Recalling definitions does not yet show independent concept selection. Choose fresh, related cases without concept labels and ask for a choice with reasons. Obtain the cases and reference explanations from a reliable course source or a tutor who can check them. Compare the actual reasoning with that reference; if a definition or prerequisite is missing, study that part before trying again.

On a later occasion, attempt a different case without the earlier hints and record the result and any help. If its answer was already seen, use another case or label the work as reconstruction. This checks later selection, rather than repetition of an exposed card. More than one plan can fit; the next interval depends on the task, performance, and deadline. Choosing and completing those subject tasks is a separate activity, not an outcome established by this planning answer.
