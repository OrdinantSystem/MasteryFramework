# Review and Retention

## Why

Immediate success can reflect short-lived fluency. Delayed **closed-notes** retrieval and mixed problems provide stronger evidence of usable learning.

## Default starting schedule (adapt, do not worship)

- **Same day:** 3–5 minute recall of definitions and a key proof step.
- **Next day:** short closed-notes problem.
- **3–4 days:** related but altered example.
- **~7 days:** mixed problems; no labels naming the technique.
- **~21–30 days:** cumulative transfer test and proof reconstruction.

Intervals are a **heuristic**, not a universal optimum. Increase spacing after reliable independent success; shorten after failure. Plan around real deadlines and workload.

## Review-session steps

1. Do not show notes or model answer first.
2. Predict performance/confidence (optional, 0–100%).
3. Attempt 1–3 varied tasks with no hints initially.
4. Compare accuracy, rigor, independence, and confidence.
5. Diagnose mistakes using [[02-protocols/04-Error-Diagnosis]].
6. Schedule only the concepts actually requiring another review.

## Interleaving

Once fundamentals exist, mix tasks from several related topics: linear independence, span, kernels, rank, etc. Ask **which framework applies and why**. Avoid mixing so early that nothing can be attempted.

## Retrieval vs reading

Reading source material *after* a failed retrieval is productive correction. Repeated reading *instead of* retrieval can produce familiarity without reliable recall. Flashcards are useful for terminology, definitions, and theorem hypotheses, but insufficient as the sole method for mathematical proofs.
