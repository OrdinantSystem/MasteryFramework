# Proof Workshop

**Goal:** develop the ability to *invent, verify, and communicate proofs*, not simply recognize a complete proof.

## Before attempting

1. Restate the proposition with quantifiers, domains, assumptions, and conclusion.
2. Translate definitions; test small cases to generate intuition **without confusing examples with proof**.
3. Ask whether an easy counterexample exists (especially for suspected converses).
4. Identify potentially useful prior definitions/theorems from the permitted syllabus.

## While attempting

- Learner writes the next logical step, not just the answer.
- Agent does **not** supply the next step unless asked or the learner is genuinely blocked.
- Check necessity of every assumption, variable scope, implication direction, and equality.
- Encourage a proof plan (direct, contradiction, contrapositive, induction, construction, invariants, etc.) but do not force a technique when several work.

## Hint ladder (record highest level used)

`H0` Independent attempt; `H1` strategic question; `H2` relevant concept/theorem; `H3` localized intermediate step; `H4` analogous worked example; `H5` complete proof. Escalate by consent. Do not call an H3 solution independent.

## After a proof

1. Audit correctness and clarity line by line.
2. Reconstruct proof independently after hiding the answer.
3. Test a variation: converse? weaker assumption? counterexample? alternative argument?
4. Log the exact obstacle and revisit after a delay.

## Example challenge patterns

- Prove an equivalence in both directions; catch missing reverse implication.
- Given a theorem, remove one hypothesis and determine whether it survives.
- Find a concrete counterexample to a false generalization.
- Give two proof strategies and compare what each reveals.
- Explain why checking 100 cases proves no universal theorem (unless a finite exhaustive domain is established).

## Quality bar

A proof is finished when another mathematically literate reader can check its assumptions and each conclusion. Good exposition is not a substitute for valid reasoning; valid reasoning also deserves readable exposition.
